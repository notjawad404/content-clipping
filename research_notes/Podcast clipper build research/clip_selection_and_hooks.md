# Clip Selection & Hooks: LLM-driven viral-moment selection for podcast clips (Reels / TikTok / Shorts, late-2026)

Evidence tiers used below: **[Official]** = Meta/Instagram/Anthropic/YouTube statements or docs; **[Data]** = large-sample analytics study; **[Vendor]** = tool-maker claims (commercial interest); **[OSS]** = open-source code/README; **[Secondary]** = press/blog summaries of official statements (primary not retrieved); **[Folklore]** = creator/clipper advice without data. Research date: 2026-11-07. Anything dated before 2025 is flagged "(pre-2025, possibly stale)".

## 1. What makes a podcast clip work as standalone short-form content

### Takeaway
Every credible source converges on the same core: an attention-grabbing opening that is on-topic, a self-contained thought that needs no prior context, and a satisfying ending/payoff. Tools then layer emotional intensity, contrarian/opinion "bombs", revelations, quotability and practical value as secondary signals. Almost nothing published by named clip teams (Hormozi, Bartlett) is primary; most "agency" guidance is vendor/folklore.

### Cited Findings
- **[Vendor] OpusClip Virality Score (0–99) is computed from four factors**: *Hook* ("whether the introduction grabs attention and relates to the video's main topic"), *Flow* ("progresses logically and ends satisfyingly"), *Value* ("offers value, resonates emotionally, and connects personally"), *Trend* ("aligns with current trends and audience interests"). Clips are sorted by score by default; ClipAnything also checks prompt relevance. — [OpusClip Help: Virality Score](https://help.opus.pro/docs/article/virality-score)
- **[Vendor] OpusClip's 2026 API blog lists detection signals** as emotional intensity, narrative completeness, hook strength, audio dynamics, and quotability (a JSON-LD FAQ version swaps quotability for "visual engagement" — the page contradicts itself). Published May 13, 2026. — [OpusClip: Viral moment detection API](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)
- **[OSS] SamurAIGPT AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator `VIRALITY_CRITERIA`** — eight signals "ranked by impact": hook moments ("statements that create immediate curiosity"), emotional peaks, opinion bombs, revelation moments, conflict/tension, quotable one-liners, story peaks, practical value. System prompt rules: strong hook in the first 3 seconds, clip must be "complete and self-contained", no significant overlap with other clips, 0–100 score, plus a `hook_sentence` and one-sentence `virality_reason`. — [highlights.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamurAIGPT/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator/main/shorts_generator/highlights.py); [repo](https://github.com/SamurAIGPT/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator)
- **[OSS] imgly/videoclipper** selection criteria: "strong hooks, complete thoughts, standalone value"; picks 3–4 clips of 30–60 s per video. — [imgly/videoclipper README](https://github.com/imgly/videoclipper)
- **[Official, pre-2025 possibly stale] Instagram's Reels ranking predicts** how likely you are to (1) reshare, (2) watch all the way through, (3) like, (4) visit the audio page. Instagram also surveys viewers on whether a reel is "worth their time or fun". (Mosseri, "Instagram Ranking Explained", May 31, 2023.) — [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)
- **[Folklore/Vendor] Hormozi-style shorts** reportedly open with a bold statement, a surprising stat, or a pain-point question, with a 1–3-word on-screen claim to carry viewers through the first ~8 seconds. No official description of Hormozi's clip team process was found. — [reap.video](https://reap.video/blog/3-simple-strategies-to-create-viral-youtube-shorts-inspired-by-alex-hormozi); [AutoClip: clipping Hormozi](https://autoclip.dev/blog/how-to-clip-alex-hormozi-content-for-clip-channels)
- **[Official-ish, platform creator blog] Spotify's creator resources** advise to "hook them in the first three to seven seconds" and to have "a few different moments that create a story arc". — [Spotify for Creators: clips drive discovery](https://creators.spotify.com/resources/grow/spotify-clips-drive-discovery)
- **[Secondary, unverified] TikTok for Business** is widely quoted as saying "over 63% of those videos with the highest click-through rate (CTR) highlight their key message or product within the first three seconds" (ads data, not organic). Other blogs quote 60% or 67% for the same idea; the primary TikTok page was not found. — [Emplicit TikTok CTR checklist](https://emplicit.co/tiktok-ctr-optimization-checklist/)
- **[Independent-ish review] Known failure mode of AI selection**: OpusClip "favors loud words, emotional tone, and keywords, and struggles with slow-building content"; users report it suggests decent sections but misses the strongest ones, and produces "awkward endings". — [BIGVU OpusClip test (Oct 2026)](https://bigvu.tv/blog/opus-clip-tested-2026-where-ai-wins-40-percent-discard/)
- **[Official] Whop Content Rewards** distinguishes *Clipping* (repurposing existing long-form: podcasts, livestreams, webinars) from *UGC*; campaign owners set the brief and creators are paid per approved view. The detailed per-campaign rules (hooks, minimum length) are set by each brand, not Whop. — [Whop docs: Content Rewards](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards)
- **[Folklore/third-party] Typical Whop clipping-campaign requirements**: comments enabled, minimum clip length "usually 15–60 seconds", bans on misleading claims/competitor mentions; pay ~$1–5 per 1k approved views (one guide: $0.20–$6). — [OpenClip Whop clipping guide 2026](https://openclip.app/guides/whop-clipping-guide); [ClipSpeed: Whop fees/holds](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/whop-content-rewards-clipping-guide.html)
- **[Secondary] Clipping campaigns are ads**; many clips fail to carry FTC-required disclosure. — [ContentGrip on clipping](https://www.contentgrip.com/clipping-creator-marketing-strategy/)

### Inferences
- **Hook taxonomy to give the LLM as an enum** (synthesized from SamurAIGPT criteria, Hormozi folklore, OpusClip): `bold_claim`, `contrarian_take`, `surprising_stat`, `story_opener` ("I was 23 and broke when…"), `direct_question`, `pain_point`, `confession/revelation`, `conflict/disagreement` (host vs guest pushback), `nobody_talks_about` / insider secret, `list/framework_promise` ("three things…"), `emotional_peak` (laughter, tears, anger), `quotable_one_liner`. Forcing the model to label the hook type makes "no real hook" visible.
- **"No context needed" should be an explicit gate, not a soft score**: reject candidates whose first ~10 s contain unresolved referents ("he", "that", "like I said", "going back to…"), inside jokes, or a question whose answer requires earlier conversation. The fix is often to start *later* (at the claim) or to stitch in a short setup sentence from elsewhere.
- **Payoff rule**: the clip must end on a resolution — punchline, answer, number, lesson, or emotional beat — not trail into the next topic. This maps directly to Instagram's "watch all the way through" prediction and OpusClip's "ends satisfyingly" Flow criterion.
- **Map rubric dimensions to the platform's actual predictions**: hook → watch-through/early retention; standalone + payoff → completion; shareability ("would you DM this to a specific person?") → reshare/sends; quotability/rewatch → absolute watch time. This anchors scoring to what Instagram says it predicts rather than to vibe.
- **Deliberately counter the known LLM/AI bias toward loud, keyword-heavy moments** (BIGVU failure mode): include in the prompt that quiet, slow-building stories with a strong payoff qualify, and that the best hook is frequently not the speaker's first sentence; it may be mid-answer.
- **Heuristic (folklore, unsourced)**: if the necessary setup exceeds ~15 s before anything interesting happens, it is a "segment" not a clip; either start at the claim and stitch the setup in after the hook, or discard.

### Gaps
- No primary, published guidance from Steven Bartlett's (Diary of a CEO) or Alex Hormozi's clip teams was found; all Hormozi claims are third-party vendor blogs.
- No controlled study comparing hook *types* (stat vs story vs question) for podcast clips on Reels; hook-type advice is folklore.
- Could not retrieve a full Whop podcast-campaign brief; requirements vary per campaign.

## 2. Ideal clip length for Instagram Reels (2025–2026) and how Reels ranking weights watch time, completion, likes, sends

### Takeaway
Best available data (Socialinsider, ~9.8M Reels, Jan–Jun 2026) shows **45–60 s Reels get the most median views and highest engagement rate**, with <30 s Reels getting less than half the views, and >3 min collapsing because they aren't recommended to non-followers. Instagram's own 2026 creator guidance calls 15–60 s the "primary sweet spot". Mosseri's top Reels signals (per secondary reports, Jan 2025) are **watch time, likes per reach, and sends per reach**, with sends mattering most for reaching non-followers. Target **~30–60 s, allow 20–90 s**, never >3 min.

### Cited Findings
**Length data and official guidance**
- **[Data] Socialinsider, 9,771,822 organic Reels, Jan–Jun 2026** (published Jul 14, 2026, updated Oct 20, 2026). Median views by length: 1–30 s **4,700**; 30–45 s 8,564; **45–60 s 10,374 (highest)**; 60–90 s 9,790; 90–120 s 8,000; 120–180 s 9,000; 180 s+ 4,428 (lowest). Engagement rate (likes+comments / followers; *excludes saves and shares*): 0–30 s 0.28%; 30–45 s 0.30%; **45–60 s 0.35%**; 60–90 s 0.30%; 90–120 s 0.30%; 120–180 s 0.33%; 180 s+ 0.15%. Recommends 45–60 s as default for reaching new audiences; notes Reels >3 min are not placed in the Reels tab or pushed to non-followers. Methodology text has internal inconsistencies about follower-tier inclusion. No completion/watch-time data reported. — [Socialinsider: Ideal Reels length](https://socialinsider.io/blog/instagram-reels-length/)
- **[Official via secondary] Instagram's 2026 creator guide** lists: 3–15 s for quick trends/punchy moments; **15–60 s as the "primary sweet spot"**; 60 s–3 min for storytelling/education. Also highlights timed on-screen text and the native auto-caption sticker. — [iPhone in Canada summarizing Instagram's 2026 guide (Sep 28, 2026)](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2026/09/28/instagram-shares-ultimate-blueprint-for-viral-reels-in-2026/)
- **[Official via secondary] Instagram advised (March 5, 2025 creator event) against posting Reels longer than 3 minutes**; the 2024 version of the same tip list said avoid >90 s. — [Social Media Today (Mar 2025)](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-reels-tips-3-minutes-unfiltered-nyc/741722/)
- **[Official, Jan 2025] Mosseri raised the Reels cap from 90 s to 3 min**: "Starting today, you can upload Reels to Instagram up to three minutes long" and "We still are focused on short form video over long form." — [Lindsey Gamble](https://www.lindseygamble.com/blog/instagram-three-minute-reels); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-instagram-reels-3-minutes-us-tiktok-ban/)
- **[Secondary] Max Reel length raised to 20 minutes (~Dec 2025)**, but multiple sources say only Reels ≤3 min are recommended to non-followers (third-party, not a direct Instagram quote). — [Inro: 20-minute Reels](https://www.inro.social/blog/instagram-reels-can-now-be-20-minutes-long-new-time-limit-explained-2025); [SocialPilot, Sept 2026](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)

**Ranking signals**
- **[Official, pre-2025 possibly stale] Signals in rough order**: your activity (likes/saves/reshares/comments), your history with the poster, info about the reel (audio, visuals, popularity), info about the poster. Predictions: reshare, watch-through, like, visit audio page. Instagram makes **less visible**: low-resolution or watermarked reels, muted reels, reels with borders, reels that are mostly text, and reels already posted on Instagram (reposts). Recommendation Guidelines (e.g., violence, regulated goods) make content ineligible for recommendation. — [Instagram Ranking Explained (May 31, 2023)](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)
- **[Secondary] Mosseri (Jan 2025) named the three most important Reels signals: watch time, likes per reach, sends per reach.** Watch time reportedly considers both relative (% viewed) and absolute (seconds). Likes matter slightly more for connected (follower) reach; **sends matter more for unconnected (non-follower) reach**. Instagram has published no numeric weights. — [Fanpage Karma](https://www.fanpagekarma.com/insights/instagram-reels-algorithm/); [Torro: 3 metrics from Mosseri](https://torro.io/blog/3-most-important-instagram-metrics-for-reach); [Hanami: what Meta has publicly shared](https://hanami-social-website.pages.dev/en/blog/instagram-reels-algorithm-2026/)
- **[Secondary, 2024 (pre-2025, possibly stale)] Mosseri said share/send rate correlates with reach more than anything else**, even more than watch time — conflicts in emphasis with the Jan 2025 framing that puts watch time first. — [SwipeInsight (May 2024)](https://web.swipeinsight.app/posts/instagram-head-share-rates-crucial-for-broad-reach)
- **[Secondary] No confirmed ranking-formula change through Sept 2026**; 2026 changes were features/policy ("Your Algorithm" topic controls launched Dec 2025 for Reels, expanded Jan/Apr/Jun 2026; originality rules extended Apr 2026). Claims like "sends weighted 3–5× likes" are unsourced. — [SocialPilot (Sept 2026)](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)
- **[Secondary] Trial Reels** (public accounts with 1,000+ followers) show a reel to non-followers first; Mosseri: "a great way to test if your content ranks highly." — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)

**TikTok / YouTube Shorts context**
- **[Official via press] YouTube Shorts views (from Mar 31, 2025) count every start/replay with no minimum watch time**; the old metric is now "engaged views", which still governs YPP eligibility and earnings. — [TechCrunch (Mar 26, 2025)](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/26/youtube-is-changing-how-youtube-shorts-views-are-counted)
- **[Secondary] TikTok Creator Rewards pays only for original videos >1 minute** (plus 10k followers, 100k views/30 days, 18+); excludes duets/stitches. Primary TikTok page not retrieved. — [TT Calculator CRP guide 2026](https://ttcalculator.net/guides/creator-rewards-program/); [ShortsFast 2026](https://shortsfast.com/blog/tiktok-creator-rewards-2026-requirements/)
- **[OSS defaults as a sanity check]**: SamurAIGPT sweet spot 45–90 s (20–44 s only for a perfect one-liner; 91–180 s only for a story arc); imgly 30–60 s; an Apify podcast clipper constrains to 20–75 s. — [highlights.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamurAIGPT/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator/main/shorts_generator/highlights.py); [imgly](https://github.com/imgly/videoclipper); [Apify podcast-clipper](https://apify.com/yasaslive/podcast-clipper.md)

### Inferences
- **Recommended length policy for the selector**: hard min 20 s, hard max 90 s for Reels (allow up to 180 s only for flagged "story" clips); **target band 30–60 s**, prefer 40–60 s when the content sustains it. Rationale: Socialinsider median views more than double from <30 s to 45–60 s; Instagram's own "sweet spot" is 15–60 s; >60 s gives little extra in views and raises completion risk.
- **Platform variants**: if the same clip will also go to TikTok and the account is in Creator Rewards, emit an optional ≥61 s cut (e.g., extend with the follow-up answer) since only >1 min videos pay there; Shorts ≤3 min.
- **Because Socialinsider's engagement metric excludes shares/sends**, and sends are the top non-follower signal, the length data under-measures what matters most for a clip page growing via non-followers. Length should be subordinate to "is every second earning its place" (watch time is both % and absolute seconds).
- **Avoid known demotion triggers in the render stage**: borders/letterboxing, watermarks from other platforms, low resolution, reels that are mostly text, muted audio.

### Gaps
- No Meta primary source (video/transcript) for Mosseri's Jan 2025 "three signals" was retrieved; all are secondary.
- No public data on Reels completion rate or rewatch weighting by length; Instagram has not published weights.
- No 2025–2026 study on podcast-clip length specifically (all studies are cross-genre brand accounts).
- The 3-minute non-follower recommendation cutoff is consistently reported but not found as a direct Instagram quote.

## 3. Structuring the LLM pass over a 1–3 hour word-timestamped transcript

### Takeaway
A 3-hour transcript fits comfortably in a single Claude context (current models: 1M tokens), so chunking is no longer required for capacity; use full-context for global awareness plus structured multi-pass refinement. The strongest pattern from open-source tools: **don't let the LLM emit raw timestamps — have it reference transcript text/IDs and map back to word timestamps in code**, then snap boundaries deterministically.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official] Current Claude models (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5, Fable 5.1) have 1M-token context windows and 128K max output.** "1M tokens is roughly 555k words" on the current tokenizer (~1.8 tokens/word); "200k tokens is roughly 150k words" (older tokenizer). — [Claude Models overview](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/models/overview)
- **[Official] Anthropic long-context guidance (20k+ tokens)**: put long documents at the top, query/instructions at the end ("Queries at the end can improve response quality by up to 30 percent in tests"); wrap documents in XML tags with metadata; "**Ground responses in quotes**": ask Claude to quote relevant parts first, then do the task; use 3–5 examples wrapped in `<example>` tags; prefill is not supported on Claude 4.6+ models; use structured outputs or explicit format instructions with retries for JSON. — [Claude prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)
- **[OSS] SamurAIGPT chunking** (built for smaller models): videos >1,800 s are split into 1,200 s chunks with 60 s overlap (step 1,140 s); chunk times are offset back before dedupe; candidates sorted by score and dropped if they overlap a kept clip by >50% of their own duration; it requests `min(max(num_clips×2, 5), natural_max, 8)` candidates per chunk where natural_max ≈ duration/90. **No boundary snapping in code**; "never cut mid-sentence" exists only as a prompt instruction. Output schema: `{"highlights":[{"title","start_time","end_time","score","hook_sentence","virality_reason"}]}`, enforced with "Respond ONLY with valid JSON" plus a retry prompt; it also runs a pre-pass classifying content type (podcast/interview/…) and density to tune the prompt. — [highlights.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamurAIGPT/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator/main/shorts_generator/highlights.py)
- **[OSS] imgly/videoclipper sends the full transcript to Gemini, which returns *text selections, not timestamps***; the app matches them against word-level timestamps (ElevenLabs Scribe / Whisper). Rationale: "LLMs are bad at outputting precise timestamps"; text matching is "more reliable than asking the LLM to output timestamps directly". — [imgly/videoclipper](https://github.com/imgly/videoclipper)
- **[OSS] podcli** (MIT, Claude/Codex-based MCP server + CLI): uses Claude to score moments, checks an episode database so it "stops resuggesting moments you already published", uses audio energy and laughter detection, and supports **multi-segment cuts that drop filler, long pauses, and tangents**; exposes a `rank_clip_candidates` tool so the agent needn't read the whole transcript. — [podcli](https://github.com/nmbrthirteen/podcli)
- **[OSS] Apify podcast-clipper** runs one structured LLM call per episode returning clips with start/end, scores and hook lines in JSON, constrained to 20–75 s. — [Apify actor](https://apify.com/yasaslive/podcast-clipper.md)
- **[OSS] FunClip (ModelScope)**: v2.0.0 (May 2024) added LLM clipping — prompt + SRT subtitles → "LLM Inference" → "AI Clip" reads timestamps from model output; prompts are user-editable (e.g., "Pick the host's 3 funniest one-liners"). For precise text-based clipping, use Paraformer since the Nano ASR checkpoint lacks reliable character-level timestamps. (pre-2025 design, possibly stale) — [FunClip mirror](https://github.com/lightjunction/funclip); [FunClip docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/modelscope/funclip)
- **[Vendor] OpusClip API separates generation from scoring**: you can submit arbitrary start/end segments to score, or auto-select top N above a threshold; it reportedly learns per-show from approved/rejected clips. — [OpusClip API blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)

### Inferences
- **Token budget**: a 2-hour podcast at the user's 25–30k-word estimate ≈ 45–55k tokens as plain text (~1.8 tokens/word); 3 hours ≈ 70–80k. Raw word-level JSON (`{"w":"…","s":…,"e":…}` per word) inflates this 5–10×, so **never feed raw word JSON to the LLM**. Feed a compact sentence table instead, e.g. one line per sentence: `S0412 [01:02:13.4–01:02:17.9] GUEST: text…` (plus a marker like `⏸1.2s` for pauses ≥0.7 s). Keep word timestamps in code only.
- **Claude Code note (observation from this environment's tool spec)**: the Read tool returns up to 2,000 lines by default, so a 3-hour transcript at one sentence per line (~2–4k lines) needs paging or a large-read option; alternatively pre-split into 2–4 files by time and read all before selecting.
- **Recommended pipeline** (synthesis):
  1. **Pass 0 (code)**: from word timestamps build sentences (punctuation + pause), speaker labels, pause-after durations, filler-word flags ("um", "uh", "you know", false starts), and optional audio energy/laughter peaks (podcli uses these).
  2. **Pass 1 (full-context candidate generation)**: transcript at top, instructions at end (Anthropic guidance). Ask for 2–3× the final number of candidates (SamurAIGPT pattern), each with **verbatim hook quote and payoff quote + start/end sentence IDs**, hook type, and one-line reason. Quote-grounding (Anthropic) doubles as the timestamp-mapping key (imgly pattern). For recall, optionally add a sweep over 20-min windows with 60 s overlap (SamurAIGPT constants) and merge/dedupe (>50% overlap → keep higher).
  3. **Pass 2 (per-candidate boundary refinement)**: give each candidate ±60–90 s of surrounding sentences; ask the model to (a) move start to the earliest sentence that is a hook and needs no prior context, (b) extend end to the payoff, (c) optionally propose up to 2–3 *stitched* sub-segments that drop tangents/filler, keeping the original order unless the reorder is a deliberate "hook-first" cold open.
  4. **Pass 3 (independent scoring/critique)**: score each refined clip on the rubric (section 4) in a fresh context without seeing the other candidates' scores; include a "devil's advocate" check: what context is missing, would a stranger understand it, does it end on a payoff.
  5. **Pass 4 (pairwise ranking of the top ~10–15)** to produce the final order.
  6. **Post-process (code)**: map sentence IDs → word timestamps; snap start to the first word onset minus ~100–200 ms, end to the last word offset plus ~200–400 ms, or to the midpoint of the next silence gap; never cut inside a word; reject cuts where the gap to the next word is <~150 ms (mid-phrase); enforce duration limits; trim internal silences >~0.6–0.8 s and filler words when the "tighten" option is on. (Millisecond thresholds are engineering heuristics, not sourced.)
- **Why full-context still beats chunk-only**: the model can avoid near-duplicate takes on the same idea, find the *best* telling of a recurring story, and stitch a setup from minute 12 onto a payoff at minute 47 only if it sees both.
- **Store published clips** (start/end, topic) and pass them in as "already used" to prevent resuggesting (podcli pattern), which also matters for Instagram's duplicate-content rules (section 6).
- **Structured output**: use JSON with a schema (structured outputs or tool-call with enums) rather than prefill, per Anthropic docs. Validate with code: increasing times, durations in range, quote strings found in transcript (fuzzy match), no >50% overlaps.

### Gaps
- No published prompt from OpusClip, Vizard, Submagic, or Klap; their pipelines are proprietary.
- No benchmark comparing full-context vs chunked selection quality on 1M-context models.
- Could not retrieve verbatim prompt text from podcli's backend or ClipsAI/OpenShorts repos (only READMEs); SamurAIGPT's prompt was paraphrased by the fetch tool rather than copied in full.

## 4. Scoring rubric design, calibration, and whether LLM virality scores predict performance

### Takeaway
Use a **gated + anchored multi-dimension rubric** whose dimensions map to what Instagram says it predicts (watch-through, reshare/sends, likes). Treat LLM scores as **a ranking for human review, not a performance prediction**. Evidence that LLM/AI virality scores predict real outcomes is modest: vendor claims (~75% above-median hit rate at 70+) are unverified, and an independent test found the best performer scored 64 while the weakest scored 87.

### Cited Findings
- **[Vendor] OpusClip claims** its model is trained on "hundreds of thousands" of (clip, performance) pairs; a 70+ score "predicts above-median performance about 75% of the time on talking-head content"; lower threshold by 10–15 points for non-talking-head; but cautions some 60-scored clips outperform 85-scored ones, so **use scores to rank candidates rather than approve them**. — [OpusClip API blog (May 2026)](https://www.opus.pro/blog/viral-moment-detection-api)
- **[Independent-ish] Test of 76 OpusClip clips (6 videos, 30 days)**: 47 (~62%) publishable as-is, 19 (~25%) needed light editing, 10 (~13%) unusable; 4 of 11 B-roll inserts had wrong context. Best-performing clip scored 64; weakest scored 87. Advice: use the score to decide what to *review first*, not what to post. (Main test by a competitor, ScaleReach — conflict of interest; tiny sample.) — [BIGVU (Oct 2026)](https://bigvu.tv/blog/opus-clip-tested-2026-where-ai-wins-40-percent-discard/)
- **[Academic] "Large Language Models Are Natural Video Popularity Predictors" (ACL 2025 Findings)**: on 13,639 videos, an LLM reasoning over titles/frame descriptions/captions reached **82% accuracy without fine-tuning** vs 80% for a supervised embedding model; combining them reached 85.5%; the LLM also produced interpretable attribute-based explanations. (Popularity classification across countries, not short-form clip selection.) — [ACL Anthology](https://preview.aclanthology.org/setup/2025.findings-acl.597)
- **[Academic] "Understanding Virality: A Rubric-based VLM Framework for Short-Form Edutainment" (arXiv 2512.21402, Dec 2025)**: VLM-extracted audiovisual features clustered into interpretable factors, fed to a regression evaluator on YouTube Shorts; reports "strong correlations between predicted and actual engagement" (no coefficients in abstract). — [arXiv 2512.21402](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21402v1)
- **[Academic] ICCV VQualA 2025 EVQA-SnapUGC challenge winner** ensembled VideoLLaMA2 + Qwen2.5-VL for short-video engagement prediction, evidence that multimodal (not transcript-only) models are state-of-the-art for engagement prediction. — [arXiv 2508.02516 summary](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2508.02516)
- **[Academic] Rubric scoring has position bias**: "Am I More Pointwise or Pairwise?" (Findings of EMNLP 2026) shows rubric-based LLM judging behaves like multiple choice, with statistically significant, model-specific bias from the **order of rubric options** across six judges and four datasets; scoring across a few balanced permutations of option order and aggregating reduces bias and **improves correlation with human judgments**. — [arXiv 2602.02219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02219v1); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-emnlp.1444/)
- **[Practitioner] Pairwise judging** is more consistent for "is A better than B" than absolute scores but has position bias (mitigate by running both orders); pointwise scores fluctuate run-to-run; stable rubrics need explicit category definitions, anchor examples, and a forced output format. (Blog opinion, not controlled study.) — [Barking Iguana: rubric you can trust](https://barkingiguana.com/writing/llm-as-a-judge-designing-a-rubric-you-can-trust/)
- **[Official] Anthropic**: 3–5 diverse, structured examples improve accuracy and consistency; ground in quotes before judging. — [Claude prompting best practices](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/prompt-engineering/claude-prompting-best-practices)

### Inferences
- **Proposed rubric (design synthesis; weights are a starting hypothesis, not evidence-derived):**
  - **Hard gates (fail → discard)**: (G1) *Standalone*: a stranger understands it with zero prior context; (G2) *Complete*: has a clear payoff/resolution; (G3) *Clean boundaries*: starts and ends on sentence boundaries; (G4) *Length* 20–90 s (story exception ≤180 s); (G5) *Recommendability*: nothing likely to violate Instagram Recommendation Guidelines (graphic violence, regulated drugs/tobacco, etc.).
  - **Scored 1–5 with written anchors** (each with a required verbatim evidence quote):
    1. *Hook strength* (first sentence / ~3 s): 5 = a specific, surprising claim/stat/question that creates an open loop; 3 = on-topic but generic; 1 = throat-clearing ("so yeah, um…"). Weight ~25%.
    2. *Payoff/ending*: 5 = punchline, number, or lesson lands in the last 3 s; 1 = trails off. ~15%.
    3. *Shareability / "send" test*: 5 = viewer would DM it to a specific person ("this is so you", "you need to hear this"), polarizing or highly relatable; 1 = nobody would forward it. ~20% (maps to sends, the top non-follower signal).
    4. *Emotional intensity / energy*: laughter, anger, vulnerability, conflict between speakers. ~10%.
    5. *Novelty / contrarian value*: non-obvious insight, counter-consensus take, insider info. ~15%.
    6. *Retention density*: every sentence advances; no dead air or tangents (or they can be stitched out). ~10%.
    7. *Quotability / rewatch*: a line that works as on-screen text or a caption. ~5%.
  - Compute total = weighted sum → 0–100; store per-dimension scores for later calibration.
- **Calibration tactics**: (a) write 2–3 anchor descriptions per score level and include 3–5 real past clips with known outcomes as examples; (b) score each candidate in isolation, in randomized order, to avoid cross-candidate anchoring; (c) for the top ~10–15, run a pairwise tournament (both orders; a win counts only if consistent); (d) randomize/permute rubric option order across 2–3 runs and average (EMNLP 2026 finding); (e) require evidence quotes before the number.
- **Close the loop**: log each posted clip's Instagram Insights (average watch time, likes/reach, sends/reach, views from non-followers) and periodically regress outcomes on per-dimension scores to re-weight. Expect transcript-only scoring to be a weak predictor (the strongest academic results use multimodal/visual features), so position the score as a triage aid.
- **Calibrate for the niche**: OpusClip's own advice to shift thresholds by content type suggests per-show/per-niche baselines rather than a global cutoff.

### Gaps
- No public, independent study correlating transcript-only LLM clip scores with Reels performance at scale.
- No controlled head-to-head of pairwise vs pointwise for this specific task; recommendations rely on general LLM-judge literature and practitioner blogs.
- OpusClip's 75% figure is a vendor claim with no published methodology.

## 5. On-screen hook text, titles, captions, hashtags, and CTAs for Reels (2025–2026)

### Takeaway
Officially confirmed: **max 5 hashtags per post/Reel (since Dec 2025)** and hashtags help search, not reach; Instagram promotes timed on-screen text and native auto-captions but demotes Reels that are "mostly text". Almost everything else (hook-text word counts, caption length, CTA wording) is folklore; design the LLM to output a short on-screen hook line, a title, and a keyword-rich caption with ≤5 relevant hashtags.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official via press] Instagram limited hashtags to 5 per post and Reel (Dec 2025)**: "We're now limiting hashtags to a maximum of five per post." Mosseri's position: hashtags help search but don't increase reach; use a few specific tags over generic lists. Note one source says the exact "not a way to get more reach" wording has not surfaced in original form. — [Business Today (Dec 19, 2025)](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/no-more-hashtag-stuffing-instagram-limits-hashtag-usage-to-just-5-per-reel-and-post-507444-2025-12-19); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-implements-new-limits-on-hashtag-use/808309/); [VOI](https://voi.id/en/amp/544834)
- **[Official via secondary] Instagram's 2026 creator guide** highlights scheduling on-screen text at set timestamps and the native auto-caption sticker; Edits app supports multi-track timelines/keyframes. — [iPhone in Canada (Sep 2026)](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2026/09/28/instagram-shares-ultimate-blueprint-for-viral-reels-in-2026/)
- **[Official, pre-2025 possibly stale] Reels that are "mostly text"**, watermarked, bordered, low-res, or muted are made less visible. — [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)
- **[Official via secondary, Apr 2026] Instagram's originality guidance cites "unique text"**, creative edits, and voiceover as ways to make third-party content original; changing speed or adding a screenshot of the original poster's name is not enough. — [Tubefilter (Apr 30, 2026)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/)
- **[Folklore/Vendor] Hormozi-style on-screen hook**: a 1–3-word claim as on-screen text to hold the first ~8 s. — [reap.video](https://reap.video/blog/3-simple-strategies-to-create-viral-youtube-shorts-inspired-by-alex-hormozi)
- **[Unsourced claim in a 2026 roundup]** "Keyword captions outperform hashtag-heavy strategies" — stated without a source. — [SocialPilot (Sept 2026)](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)
- **[OSS] Output fields used by open-source clippers**: `title`, `hook_sentence`, `virality_reason` (SamurAIGPT); hook lines (Apify). — [highlights.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/SamurAIGPT/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator/main/shorts_generator/highlights.py); [Apify](https://apify.com/yasaslive/podcast-clipper.md)

### Inferences
- **Have the LLM emit four distinct text artifacts per clip**:
  1. `onscreen_hook` (persistent top-of-frame text for the first ~3–5 s): 3–8 words, a claim/curiosity gap that *complements* (not duplicates) the spoken first line, e.g. "He lost $40M in one day". This is also the "unique text" that helps the originality case.
  2. `title` (for Shorts/TikTok title fields and internal tracking): ≤60 characters, specific, no clickbait that the clip doesn't pay off (payoff mismatch hurts completion).
  3. `caption`: first line restates the hook/keyword phrase (searchable), 1–3 short lines of context, credit line for the original podcast/guest (attribution), **≤5 niche-specific hashtags**.
  4. `cta` (optional): a send-oriented prompt matched to the clip ("send this to someone who…") targets the sends signal; treat as an A/B variable since no evidence was found that CTAs help or that Instagram penalizes them.
- **Burned-in captions** (word-by-word subtitles) are standard practice and support muted viewing; keep them in the safe zone and avoid full-screen text that could read as "mostly text".
- Generate 2–3 alternative `onscreen_hook` variants per clip for Trial Reels testing (section 6).

### Gaps
- No reliable 2025–2026 data on optimal caption length, on-screen text length, or CTA effectiveness for Reels.
- No official Instagram statement on whether "send this to…" CTAs are treated as engagement bait.
- TikTok/YouTube Shorts title/caption best practices for 2026 were not researched in depth.

## 6. Running a clip page that reposts other creators' podcasts: originality rules, niche, posting frequency, variations

### Takeaway
The biggest risk for a podcast clip page on Instagram is **not volume but originality**: accounts that mostly repost others' content become ineligible for recommendations to non-followers (rules from 2022–2024, extended to photos/carousels Apr 2026). Duplicate segments the source podcast already posted lose to the original. Volume itself shows no measured penalty (Buffer: reach per post *rises* up to 10+/week), but there is no data for 3–20 posts/day. Clips must be meaningfully transformed (unique on-screen text, editing, commentary/voiceover, stitching), and permissioned/paid campaigns are safer.

### Cited Findings
- **[Official via press, 2022 (pre-2025, possibly stale)] Mosseri**: Instagram will value original work over reposts; content edited outside the app is not penalized ("if you made it, it's original"); Instagram uses classifiers estimating originality and can't know for sure. — [Engadget (2022)](https://www.engadget.com/instagrams-algorithm-overhaul-will-reward-original-content-and-penalize-aggregators-130018977.html)
- **[Official via press, Apr 30, 2024 (pre-2025, possibly stale)]** When identical content appears, only the original is recommended (reposts get a label linking to the source); accounts that repeatedly post others' content (reported as 10+ times in 30 days) are removed from recommendations; reported exception for accounts with permission to repost. These specifics (especially the 10-in-30-days number and the permission exception) come from search-result summaries of 2024 coverage and were not verified on the article page or on Instagram's site. — [Gigazine (May 2, 2024)](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20240502-instagram-updated-algorithm-prioritizes-original-content)
- **[Official via press, Apr 30, 2026] Originality penalties extended to photos and carousels**; accounts that repost too often become ineligible for algorithmic recommendations (Feed and Explore/Discover); followers still see posts. Aggregators can keep operating "if they meaningfully transform" posts; penalties target "unoriginal content that [reposters] didn't enhance". Speed changes or adding a screenshot of the original poster's name don't count; examples of originality: humor, social commentary, cultural references, "unique text, creative edits, or voiceover"; make third-party content "unmistakably theirs". No numeric threshold in this article. — [Tubefilter (Apr 30, 2026)](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/04/30/instagram-removes-algorithm-recommendations-repost-content-aggregator/)
- **[Secondary] Accounts mostly posting reposts within a rolling 30-day window are classed as "aggregator accounts"** with reduced reach across Reels, Explore, Feed (non-followers only); a claimed "70% fingerprint threshold" is unsourced. — [SocialPilot (Sept 2026)](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)
- **[Official, pre-2025 possibly stale] "Reels already posted on Instagram (reposts)" are made less visible.** — [Instagram Ranking Explained](https://about.instagram.com/blog/announcements/instagram-ranking-explained)
- **[Data] Buffer, 2.1M feed posts (photos, Reels, carousels) from ~102k accounts (Aug 2025)**: within-account fixed-effects follower growth +0.12% (1–2 posts/wk), +0.26% (3–5), +0.44% (6–9), **+0.66% (10+)**; median reach per post vs 1–2/wk: +12% (3–5), +18% (6–9), **+24% (10+)**; diminishing returns; **no measured penalty for high volume** (no cannibalization/unfollow effect found); zero-post weeks show a growth penalty. Reels not analyzed separately; correlational vendor data. — [Buffer (2025/2026)](https://buffer.com/library/how-often-to-post-on-instagram/)
- **[Official via secondary] Mosseri on frequency**: no one-size answer; successful accounts range from one post a month to five Reels a day; generally, more posts → more reach. — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/how-often-you-should-post-on-instagram/)
- **[Secondary] Trial Reels** (1,000+ followers) let you show a reel to non-followers first; Mosseri: "a great way to test if your content ranks highly." — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)
- **[Secondary] "Your Algorithm" controls (Dec 2025 → 2026)** let users add/remove topics across Reels/Explore/Feed, increasing the value of clear topical consistency. — [SocialPilot](https://www.socialpilot.co/?p=563048)
- **[Official] Whop clipping campaigns** are brand-sanctioned (the creator/podcast commissions clips), with payment per approved view. — [Whop docs](https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/growth-marketing/content-rewards)

### Inferences
- **Originality-safe selection rules for the clipper**:
  - Prefer segments the source podcast's official accounts have **not** already clipped (identical content → original wins). If the podcast channel's Shorts/Reels list can be fetched, pass "already clipped by source" timestamps to the LLM as exclusions, same as podcli's "already published" list.
  - Bake transformation into every render: unique on-screen hook text, word-level captions, tightening/stitching cuts, reframing/zooms, optional commentary or voiceover intro. Speed changes, borders, or a credit screenshot alone are explicitly insufficient.
  - Prefer **permissioned sources** (Whop/Content Rewards campaigns, Collabs, direct permission), which earlier coverage reported as an exception; keep proof of permission.
- **Volume**: there's no evidence that posting volume itself is penalized; the binding constraints are (a) the repost-ratio/originality classifier, and (b) quality dilution. A practical start for a new page: 1–3 high-scoring clips/day, scaling only while average watch time and sends/reach hold. Data on 3–20/day pages is absent, so treat higher volumes as an experiment, not a rule.
- **Variations of the same clip**: don't repost the same clip on the same account (reposts are demoted). Test hook/text variants via **Trial Reels** instead of duplicate posts; across multiple accounts, assume Instagram's duplicate detection may treat identical renders as the same content, so vary the cut (different start, stitched setup, different hook text) rather than just re-uploading.
- **Niche**: pick 1 niche per account (e.g., business/money, fitness/health, relationships, comedy) and 2–5 source podcasts within it; topical consistency helps the "Your Algorithm" topic system and connected-reach likes. The LLM prompt should include the account's niche and audience so "shareability" is judged for that audience.
- **Disclosure**: if the clip is part of a paid campaign, add the required ad/partnership disclosure (FTC).

### Gaps
- Unattributed 2026 coverage (search summaries only; source page not pinned) claims simple borders or credit lines no longer satisfy the guidelines and that recommendation eligibility returns after ~30 days of original posting. Unverified.
- No primary Instagram text found specifying the exact repost threshold (the "10 in 30 days" figure is from 2024 secondary coverage; the 2026 article gives no number).
- No data on posting 3–20 Reels/day for clip/aggregator accounts, or on multi-account networks.
- No official statement on whether transcribed captions + reframing count as "meaningful transformation" for podcast clips specifically.
- TikTok and YouTube Shorts originality/reused-content policies for clip channels were not researched in depth (YouTube's "reused content" monetization policy and TikTok's originality requirement for Creator Rewards are relevant follow-ups).
